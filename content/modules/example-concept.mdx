Shield faces are not zones — they are directional absorption pools. When incoming fire hits a face, that pool takes the load until it depletes. The regeneration rate is fixed regardless of how many faces you are running. Managing which face takes damage is the pilot's job.

The most common mistake is treating shields as a passive health bar. They are not. An engineer calling a face at 20% is giving the pilot actionable geometry information. The correct response is a break turn to present a fresh face — not to wait for regen.

Face geometry changes with your attitude relative to the threat. A target sitting at your 4 o'clock is not hitting your rear face unless your nose is pointed well away from them. Pilots must internalize their threat bearing constantly, not just when shields drop.

Distributed versus stacked management is a crew decision made before engagement. Stacked shields give you a deep pool on one face and accept death on the others. Distributed gives you balanced coverage but shallower pools across all faces. Neither is universally correct — it depends on the engagement geometry and how many threats you are managing simultaneously.
